# Literature synthesis: exploitable rules for a 3–4 week, 237-market midterm prediction-market tournament

Setting assumptions: binary House/Senate/Governor contracts, $100k play money, resolution ≈ 2026-11-03, order-book style market populated mostly by students (thin, likely partisan, probably no/low fees, limit orders available). Today = 2026-10-10.

## A. Market-level biases and calibration

### A1. Favorite-longshot bias (FLB): direction, size, dependence on price and horizon

- **Snowberg & Wolfers (2010), *JPE* 118(4)** – FLB is driven by probability misperception (prospect-theory weighting), not risk-love; it is a property of how people perceive small probabilities, so it survives in markets with no bookmaker take. [NBER w15923](https://www.nber.org/papers/w15923), [JPE](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/abs/10.1086/655844)
- **Page & Clemen (2013), *Economic Journal* 123(568): 491–513** – 500k+ Intrade transactions, 1,787 markets. Calibration is good at short horizons and deteriorates with time-to-expiration: high-probability contracts underpriced, low-probability overpriced; prices are pulled toward 0.50 as horizon lengthens. Later citers put the long-dated distortion at roughly 4.7–10.9 pp; exploitable only if your discount rate is low. [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1468-0297.2012.02561.x), [open copy](https://eprints.qut.edu.au/218538/)
- **Le (2026), "Decomposing Crowd Wisdom: Domain-Specific Calibration Dynamics in Prediction Markets", arXiv:2602.19520** – 353M Kalshi+Polymarket trades. The most robust pattern is persistent *underconfidence in political markets* (prices compressed toward 50%), replicating on both platforms; calibration slopes 0.93–1.83 across horizons. Worked example from the paper: a 70¢ politics contract one week before resolution implies ≈83% true probability. Large political trades on Kalshi are even more compressed. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.19520)
- **Bürgi, Deng & Whelan (2025), "Makers and Takers: The Economics of the Kalshi Prediction Market", UCD WP 2025/19 / CESifo 12122** – takers lose ≈32% on average, makers ≈10%; low-priced contracts win less often than break-even, high-priced contracts win more often and earn small positive returns; a 20% event is priced ≈23%. [UCD PDF](https://www.ucd.ie/economics/t4media/WP2025_19.pdf), [VoxEU summary](https://cepr.org/voxeu/columns/economics-kalshi-prediction-market)
- **Kalshi Research (2026), "Calibration in Prediction Markets: Theory and Evidence"** – 2.24M resolved markets; Brier ≈0.08–0.09 at 3 months → ≈0.02 at close; naive accuracy 88.3% → 97.2%. Secondary summary: close-horizon Brier 0.0635 for markets under $10k volume vs 0.0099 at ≥$200k (thin markets are much worse calibrated). [PDF](https://kalshi.com/research/kalshi-research-calibration.pdf)
- **Cardozo & Rivero-Wildemauwe (2026), "The Favorite–Longshot Bias in Prediction Markets: Evidence from Polymarket", arXiv:2609.12878** – 588M trades. Aggregate FLB exists but its magnitude/sign depends on weighting (equal-weight per child market and dollar-weighted show losses on longshots; equal-weight per parent event shows gains). Secondary report (unverified): dollar-weighted buys below 10¢ lost ≈19.3¢ per dollar; sports longshots were the exception. A search snippet also attributed to this study short-horizon longshot returns of +4.9%/+7.0%/+6.6% at 5 min/1 h/1 day before −36% at resolution (unverified; treat as suggestive: longshots are bought by momentum but die at resolution). [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12878)
- **Whelan (2023), *Quantitative Finance* 23(11)** – commission structures alone generate a post-fee FLB even when pre-fee prices are unbiased; Intrade's bias was "relatively small and largely confined to very long-dated contracts". [T&F](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/14697688.2023.2257756)
- **Berg & Rietz (2019), *IJF* 35(1): 271–287** – IEM (real money, no fees, academic traders): *no* longshot bias; efficient at short horizons; transitory *over*-confidence (reverse-longshot) at intermediate horizons. [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0169207018300499)
- **Zitzewitz (2026), "Limits to Arbitrage and Prediction Market Efficiency: the Case of PredictIt", NBER w35845** – with an $850 cap, bid prices "often persistently added to more than 110" and longshot overpricing was larger than in any other studied market: position limits and thin liquidity make FLB *bigger*. [NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35845)
- **Akey, Grégoire, Harvie & Martineau (2026), "Who Wins and Who Loses in Prediction Markets? Evidence from Polymarket", CEPR DP21615 / SSRN 6443103** – top 1% of profitable users capture 76.5% of profits; winners provide liquidity with limit orders that resolve favorably; losers take liquidity with market orders at extreme prices. [CEPR](https://cepr.org/publications/dp21615), [SSRN](https://ssrn.com/abstract=6443103)

**=> Trading implication.** In political markets with 1–4 weeks left, the consistent direction is *favorites underpriced / longshots overpriced*, and the effect is larger in thin, capped, fee-bearing, amateur markets (exactly this tournament). Working estimate for a student market: contracts at 70–90¢ are 3–8¢ cheap; 90–97¢ contracts 1–4¢ cheap; contracts <10¢ are 2–8¢ rich. Harvest it by *buying favorites and selling longshots with resting limit orders*, never by market-ordering into longshots. Expect roughly 3–7% per unit over the 3 weeks on the 85–97¢ book, nearly risk-free except for correlated national-swing tail risk.

### A2. Time-to-resolution and convergence ("theta")

- Page & Clemen (2013) and Kalshi (2026) above: bias shrinks monotonically as expiry approaches; Brier falls ~4x from 3 months to close. The 3-week window is where most residual compression toward 50¢ unwinds.
- **Rothschild (2009), *POQ* 73(5): 895–916** – 2008 presidential and Senate state races. Debiased market prices beat debiased polls, most of all early and in non-certain races. Polls "overstate margins" and have an anti-incumbency bias; markets have FLB ("a mean probability of 95 may translate into a price of 85"). Debiasing uses the Leigh et al. (2007) transformation; my recollection is Pr = Φ(1.64·Φ⁻¹(price)) – I could not verify the constant from the snippets, so check before coding it. [OUP](https://academic.oup.com/poq/article/73/5/895/1868587), [PDF](https://researchdmr.com/files/ForecastingElectionMarketsPolls.pdf)
- **Erikson & Wlezien (2008), *POQ* 72(2): 190–215** – raw markets beat raw polls, but polls adjusted for known biases (discounting early leads toward the fundamentals) match or beat markets; markets add little beyond adjusted polls. Their 2012 *Electoral Studies* update found markets ahead in 2008/2012. [DOI 10.1093/poq/nfn010](https://doi.org/10.1093/poq/nfn010)
- **Berg, Nelson & Rietz (2008), *IJF* 24(2): 283–298** – IEM closer to the outcome than polls 74% of the time across 964 polls 1988–2004; advantage largest >100 days out; near election day polls and markets converge. [PDF](https://www.biz.uiowa.edu/faculty/trietz/papers/long%20run%20accuracy.pdf)
- **Rothschild & Sethi (2016), *J. Prediction Markets* 10(1): 1–29** – Intrade 2012, 6,300 accounts: most directional traders bet consistently in one direction; evidence of a single large manipulator. [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2322420)
- Anecdote on failure to converge under caps: PredictIt Biden-wins-Georgia contracts traded 88–90¢ *after certification* in 2020. [Social Science Encyclopedia](https://www.socialscience.international/aiden-singh-predictit-inefficiencies)

**=> Trading implication.** With no interest on play money, the "cost of carry" objection to theta harvesting disappears; the only cost is bankroll opportunity cost. Rank all 237 contracts by (model fair value − price)/days-to-resolution and fill the book with the highest-yield near-certain contracts first; expect prices to converge to 97–99¢ only in the final 48 hours, so be prepared to hold to resolution.

### A3. When prices are not probabilities (Manski; Wolfers & Zitzewitz)

- **Manski (2006), *Economics Letters* 91(3): 425–429** – with risk-neutral price-taking traders with heterogeneous beliefs, price is a *quantile* of the belief distribution, not the mean; mean belief can lie in a wide band around price (my recollection of his bound is [π², 2π−π²], i.e., a 50¢ price is consistent with mean beliefs from 25% to 75% – verify). [NBER w10359](https://www.nber.org/papers/w10359)
- **Wolfers & Zitzewitz (2006), NBER w12200** – with log utility, price ≈ wealth-weighted mean belief and the deviation is small; prices are "useful (albeit sometimes biased) estimates of average beliefs". Dispersion of beliefs and low risk aversion widen the deviation. [NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w12200)

**=> Trading implication.** Play money + students = near risk-neutral, highly dispersed, partisan beliefs: the Manski regime. Do not read the tournament's prices as probabilities; treat them as the marginal trader's quantile and price every contract from your own model. Expect prices to sit too close to 50¢ wherever beliefs are dispersed (contested races) – consistent with Le (2026).

### A4. Martingale/arbitrage view of forecast dynamics (Taleb)

- **Taleb (2018), "Election predictions as martingales: an arbitrage approach", *Quantitative Finance* 18(1); arXiv:1703.06351** – a probability forecast is a binary-option price; when much uncertainty remains it should sit near 50% and move little; large swings imply arbitrageable "excess volatility". **Clayton (2019), *Quantitative Finance* 19(11): 1771–1774** rebuts: under mild assumptions any forecast is arbitrage-free, and Taleb's heuristic is false. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.06351), [Clayton](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/14697688.2019.1639802)

**=> Trading implication.** The usable kernel survives Clayton: a binary's price can only move as fast as the *underlying margin* can plausibly move. With σ_margin ≈ 6–7 pts for a Senate race in the final 3 weeks (Section D), a 1-point poll surprise should move a 50/50 race ≈5–6¢, not 15¢. Compute the implied margin shift of every >8¢ daily move; if it exceeds what the news could plausibly do to the margin (1–2 pts for a poll, ~0 for a gaffe), fade it.

### A5. Partisan bias / wishful thinking

- **Forsythe, Nelson, Neumann & Wright (1992), *AER* 82(5): 1142–1161** – IEM 1988: traders exhibit false-consensus/wishful-thinking biases but prices are set by marginal (limit-order) traders; **Forsythe, Rietz & Ross (1999), *JEBO*** "Wishes, expectations and actions" documents the wishful-thinking effect directly. [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/article/aeaaecrev/v_3a82_3ay_3a1992_3ai_3a5_3ap_3a1142-61.htm), [PDF](https://www.biz.uiowa.edu/faculty/trietz/papers/wishes.pdf)
- **Becker, McGurk & LoGrasso (2025), *Scottish J. Political Economy* 72(4)** – PredictIt 2020, 10 swing states: partisan sentiment in each market's comment board predicts mispricing, strongest for Republican contracts. [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/sjpe.70007)
- **Zitzewitz (2026), "Are Prediction Markets Politically Biased?", NBER w35846** – 1880–2025 returns: no overpricing of left- or right-leaning candidates in any subsample; FLB present in most political markets; a tiny left overpricing only in polling-average markets. [NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35846)
- 2022 midterms: markets (PredictIt, Betfair etc.) priced Republicans too high – Masters 53¢ vs Kelly 48¢ a week out despite polls; Oz favored in PA; all markets had GOP favored for Senate control. [Forbes/Calhoun 2022](https://www.forbes.com/sites/georgecalhoun/2022/11/14/the-un-wisdom-of-crowds-prediction-markets-failed-their-midterm-exams/), [Deseret](https://www.deseret.com/2022/11/1/23435614/betting-markets-predictit-forecast-2022-elections/)
- 2024: four Polymarket whale accounts held >41M Trump shares (~$26M) (WSJ via [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2024/10/18/presidential-election-odds-pro-trump-bettors-polymarket-political-polls-harris)); **Clinton & Huang (2025), SocArXiv** find Polymarket's moves poorly correlated with Kalshi/PredictIt/IEM, 58% of its national markets negatively serially correlated, and cross-exchange arbitrage peaking in the final two weeks. [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/p/osf/socarx/d5yx2_v1.html)

**=> Trading implication.** There is no reliable *a priori* partisan direction (2022 markets were too Republican; 2024 Polymarket was Trump-heavy and right). But *within a given trader population* the skew is real and measurable. In week 1, regress (price − your fair value) on party of the contract across all 237 markets; if the residual is significant (a Penn student cohort plausibly over-prices Democrats by 2–5¢), short that basket with limit orders. Re-estimate weekly; the skew may flip as better-informed traders arrive.

### A6. Sum-of-probabilities, cross-market consistency, Dutch books

- Zitzewitz (2026, w35845): PredictIt bids summing >110 persisted for long periods.
- **Saggese et al. / IMDEA (2025), "Unravelling the Probabilistic Forest: Arbitrage in Prediction Markets", AFT 2025; arXiv:2508.03474** – Polymarket averaged ~100 arbitrage opportunities per market; both short (ΣYES>1) and long (ΣYES<1) violations; execution is non-atomic, so there is leg risk. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.03474)
- **Gebele, Mutzel & Matthes (2026), "Executable Arbitrage and Market Efficiency in Prediction Markets", arXiv:2608.00666** – ≈$1.12M executable arb profit reconstructed; violations cluster on whichever side the protocol cannot natively convert, i.e., structural frictions determine where mispricing sits. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00666)
- Senate/House *control* vs *seat* consistency: no academic study found; practitioner commentary notes seat markets implying a Dem majority while the control contract sat at 54% GOP ([DeFi Rate](https://defirate.com/news/senate-control-play-polls-tighten-prediction-markets-mixed-messages/)). Control probability is not a function of seat prices alone – it needs the correlation structure – but it is bounded: if Dems must win a specific set S of k seats, P(control) ≤ min_{i∈S} p_i and ≥ max(0, Σ_{i∈S} p_i − (k−1)) (Fréchet), and with a national-swing factor the true value sits well above the independence product.

**=> Trading implication.** Build a consistency scanner that runs every few minutes: (i) YES-ask + NO-ask < 100 → buy both; YES-bid + NO-bid > 100 → sell both; (ii) multi-candidate events: Σ asks < 100 / Σ bids > 100; (iii) control contracts vs the correlated-simulation implied value and Fréchet bounds. In a student market expect many violations in the first days and after news shocks; they will be worth a few cents each but are riskless if both legs fill, so execute with simultaneous limit orders and size them large.

### A7. Herding, momentum vs mean-reversion, poll overreaction

- **Clinton & Huang (2025)** – daily changes weakly or negatively autocorrelated in 2024; spikes typically reversed next day.
- **Tsang & Yang (2026), "Political Shocks and Price Discovery in Prediction Markets", arXiv:2603.03152** – Polymarket 2024: the Biden–Trump *debate* jump mostly reversed; the *assassination-attempt* repricing persisted; Biden's *dropout* caused heavy two-sided volume with little net move; trading spikes come from incumbent traders. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03152)
- **Brown, Reade & Vaughan Williams (2019), "When are prediction market prices most informative?", *IJF* 35(1)** – Intrade 2008–2012 matched to Gallup releases: poll releases spike volume; the first wave is inexperienced traders reacting to the *incidence* of a poll rather than its content; price efficiency dips and recovers over the following hours as experienced traders arrive. [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0169207018300852)
- **Angelini & De Angelis (2026), "When Do Markets Fully Process Public Information?", arXiv:2606.07811** – live Kalshi markets: only ≈0.64-for-1 contemporaneous pass-through of benchmark probability changes, with predictable drift afterward; drift is larger in thin markets. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.07811)
- **Vojtko & Dujava (2026), SSRN 6726362** – mean-reversion on Polymarket binaries is profitable only with passive limit-order execution; aggressive orders destroy the edge. [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=6726362)
- **Gelman, Goel, Rivers & Rothschild (2016), "The Mythical Swing Voter", *QJPS* 11(1): 103–130** – most apparent poll swings are differential nonresponse, not opinion change. [Now](https://www.nowpublishers.com/article/Details/QJPS-15031)
- **Rhode & Strumpf (2008), NBER w14377 and the manipulation field experiment** – manipulations (IEM 2000 experiment, TradeSports 2004, 1880–1944 markets) moved prices only briefly before reverting. [NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w14377)

**=> Trading implication.** Two regimes. *Soft* information (single polls, debates, gaffes, big one-sided orders): fade with resting limit orders 3–6¢ away from the pre-shock price; expect reversal within hours to 2 days. *Hard* structural information (dropout, death, indictment, ballot disqualification, major endorsement in a primary-like context): the market under-reacts on impact (~60% pass-through) and drifts, so take the remaining ~40% by following. Classify the event before acting; the single most costly error is fading hard news.

## B. Sizing and bankroll for a short-horizon tournament with correlated binaries

- **Kelly for a binary** bought at price p with belief q: f* = (q − p)/(1 − p) of bankroll (selling YES at p with belief q: f* = (p − q)/p). **MacLean, Thorp & Ziemba (2011), *The Kelly Capital Growth Investment Criterion* (World Scientific)**: full Kelly gives a 50% chance of a 50% drawdown (Thorp); half-Kelly keeps ~75% of the growth rate at a fraction of the variance. [SSRN overview](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=1797366)
- **Whitrow (2007), *JRSS-C* 56(5): 607–623** – numerical (stochastic-gradient/simplex) maximization of expected log wealth across many simultaneous bets; stakes ≈ Kelly for few bets but diverge (shrink) as the number grows. [Wiley](https://rss.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1467-9876.2007.00594.x). **Busseti, Ryu & Boyd (2016), "Risk-constrained Kelly gambling"** – add a drawdown-probability constraint; dominates fractional Kelly at equal drawdown risk. [PDF](https://stanford.edu/~boyd//papers/pdf/kelly.pdf)
- **Correlated election outcomes.** FiveThirtyEight assumes polling errors correlated across races via demographic/geographic permutations and uses a t-distribution with 5 df ([538 2024 methodology](https://abcnews.com/538/538s-2024-presidential-election-forecast-works/story?id=113068753)); **Silver's FLIPR (2026)** uses four error layers: uniform national, demographic/geographic, state-level shared across House/Senate/Gov, and race-specific; fit on Senate since 1990, House/Gov since 1998; 40,000 sims ([Silver Bulletin](https://www.natesilver.net/p/flipr-midterms-model-methodology)). **Heidemanns, Gelman & Morris (2020), *HDSR*** (Economist model) and **Linzer (2013), *JASA* 108(501): 124–134** use hierarchical state/national error terms ([HDSR](https://hdsr.mitpress.mit.edu/pub/nw1dzd02)). **Lauderdale & Linzer (2015), *IJF* 31(3): 965–979**: honest fundamentals-only 95% intervals are ±10 pts at state level, ±7 national ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0169207015000102)). DDHQ (2022, *HDSR*) notes correlation handling has "no accepted solution" ([HDSR](https://hdsr.mitpress.mit.edu/pub/ftwzgrij/release/3)).
- **Tournament incentives.** **Ottaviani & Sørensen (2006), *JFE* 81(2)** – winner-take-all contests produce excessively differentiated forecasts ([PDF](https://web.econ.ku.dk/sorensen/papers/spf.pdf)); **Lichtendahl & Winkler (2007), *Management Science* 53(11)** – rank-based payoffs push reported probabilities toward 0/1 ([SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=961001)); **Browne (1999), *Adv. Appl. Prob.* 31: 551–577** – the policy maximizing P(reach a wealth goal by a deadline) is a digital-option replication: bet more aggressively the farther you are from the goal, less as you approach it ([SSRN](https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.703)). Poundstone's *Fortune's Formula* (2005) is the popular account. I could not identify a "Kaplan" tournament paper; treat that reference as unconfirmed.

**=> Trading implication / sizing policy (prize structure unknown).**
1. Model the portfolio, not the bets: simulate outcomes with a national shock (σ ≈ 2.5–3 pts on margin), a state shock shared across that state's races (σ ≈ 2–3), and race-specific noise (σ ≈ 4–6; larger for House), t(5) tails. Effective number of independent bets across 237 markets is on the order of 10–20, not 237.
2. Default (weeks 1–2, rank unknown): 0.25–0.5 Kelly per contract from the simulation, plus a hard cap that a 4-point uniform swing against your net party exposure costs ≤20–25% of bankroll. Near-certain favorites (≥90¢) can be sized far above Kelly-per-bet because their loss is almost purely the national-tail event the cap already controls.
3. Final week: if the payoff is rank-based and you are behind the leaders, deliberately take a concentrated, correlated national-swing position (Browne/Ottaviani logic – variance is your friend only when behind); if ahead, hedge the national factor (buy both sides of correlated sets or hold cash) and keep only the consistency arbs and theta book.
4. Never market-order size; the Kalshi/Polymarket loser profile is exactly "take liquidity at extreme prices".

## C. Social-media sentiment as an election signal

- **Tumasjan, Sprenger, Sandner & Welpe (2010), ICWSM** – tweet *volume* share matched 2009 German party vote shares (MAE ≈1.65). **Jungherr, Jürgens & Schoen (2012), *SSCR* 30(2): 229–234** – the result depends on excluding the Pirate Party and on the time window; including all parties the prediction fails. **Metaxas, Mustafaraj & Gayo-Avello (2011), IEEE SocialCom, "How (not) to predict elections"** – published Twitter methods on 2010 US Senate races were no better than chance and worse than the trivial incumbency baseline. **Gayo-Avello (2012), arXiv:1204.6441 ("…all I got was this lousy paper") and (2013), *SSCR* 31(6): 649–679 (meta-analysis)** – predictive power "somewhat exaggerated"; no real forecasts (all post-hoc); need baselines. [Metaxas](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/220876090_How_Not_to_Predict_Elections), [Gayo-Avello 2012](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1204.6441), [2013](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/0894439313493979)
- **Skoric, Liu & Jaidka (2020), *Information* 11(4): 187** – meta-analysis of 74 studies: accuracy varies widely and on average falls short of survey benchmarks; ML stance classifiers beat lexicons; structural features + sentiment combined do best. [MDPI](https://doi.org/10.3390/info11040187)
- **Beauchamp (2017), *AJPS* 61(2): 490–503** – 1,200 state polls (2012) + 100M geolocated political tweets; regularized regression on word features tracks and *interpolates/extrapolates* polls to unpolled states and sub-day resolution, beating ML benchmarks; the text is trained on polls, not used as a free-standing predictor. [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/ajps.12274)
- **Bovet, Morone & Makse (2018), *Scientific Reports* 8: 8673** – 2016: Twitter opinion (13–21-day moving average) tracks the NYT poll average with r = 0.93–0.96, RMSE 0.23–0.31 pts, with Twitter ≈10 days *ahead* of published polls (polls are fielded and released with lag); both still called Clinton. [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-018-26951-y)
- **Caballero (2021), arXiv:2107.09640** – 2020 Twitter sentiment + structure model underperformed the polling baseline. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.09640)
- **Brown, Reade & Vaughan Williams (2019)** (above) – traders react to poll *incidence*; **Sultan & Morstatter (2026), arXiv:2609.28965** – on 79 Polymarket markets, stance against the leader appears before a leadership change in 23/27 markets, but adding stance did not improve reversal forecasts beyond market-state features; comment toxicity predicted buying direction above chance in 43/53 markets. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28965)
- LLM stance detection: GPT-4o/Gemini/Claude consensus agreed with human majority >90% on 2024 X political stance ([arXiv:2412.02712](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.02712)); PolitiSky24 Bluesky stance dataset ([arXiv:2506.07606](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07606)); Political Analysis "Stay Tuned" on LLM stance ([Cambridge](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/political-analysis/article/stay-tuned-improving-sentiment-analysis-and-stance-detection-using-large-language-models/2D8F121012D3D1CB2259B6DD5EE32D0D)).
- Bots: **Bessi & Ferrara (2016), *First Monday* 21(11)** – ≈15% of accounts and ≈20% of tweets in the 2016 election conversation were bots. [First Monday](https://firstmonday.org/ojs/index.php/fm/article/view/7090)
- Platform skews (Pew): 69% of left-leaning news influencers joined Bluesky vs 15% of conservatives, 82% remain on X ([Pew 2025](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/05/29/bluesky-has-caught-on-with-many-news-influencers-but-x-remains-popular/)); among party members, 14% of Republicans vs 10% of Democrats regularly get news on X, 7% vs 12% on Reddit ([Pew fact sheet](https://www.pewresearch.org/journalism/fact-sheet/social-media-and-news-fact-sheet/)). Unverified social-media claim of X news consumers 56R/42D.
- Timing: one non-peer-reviewed bot project found RSS/news feeds trail the first X post by a median ≈32 minutes on 5 stories ([GitHub](https://github.com/mehek-builds/prediction-market)); no rigorous study of news-to-price latency in political markets found.

**=> Trading implication.** Sentiment *levels* do not forecast winners (every rigorous test fails the incumbency/poll baseline), so do not price races from sentiment. What survives: (1) *changes* in stance share, debiased against a per-platform, per-race trailing baseline, track opinion with a lead of days over published polls (Bovet et al.; Beauchamp); (2) social streams detect *events* (dropouts, scandals, endorsements, ballot rulings) tens of minutes before wire/RSS and probably hours before a thin student market reprices; (3) crowd discussion turns against a leader shortly before reversals, weakly. Operationalize as an alerting system, not a forecaster: bot-filter, classify *stance* (not sentiment) with an LLM, z-score daily stance share per race per platform against the prior 14 days, and only trade on multi-platform spikes that map to an identifiable hard event. Evaluate with (a) lead–lag cross-correlation of signal changes vs price changes at lags of 1–24 h, (b) an information coefficient (Spearman of signal vs next-day price change and vs resolution residual), (c) event studies on flagged spikes. Expect a low but nonzero IC (~0.02–0.05) and a handful of valuable alerts over 3 weeks.

## D. Fundamentals-based fair value in the final month

- **Polling error magnitudes (margin, final ~3 weeks).** FiveThirtyEight all-cycle weighted averages: Governor 5.2, Senate 6.1, House 9.3 (older table); since 1998: Senate 5.4, House 6.3 ([538 2022](https://fivethirtyeight.com/features/how-accurate-have-senate-polls-been-and-what-could-that-mean-for-november/)); 2021–22 cycle 4.8 overall – lowest on record – with "almost no bias" (slight underestimate of Democrats) ([538](https://fivethirtyeight.com/features/2022-election-polling-accuracy), [538 forecast review](https://fivethirtyeight.com/features/how-our-2022-midterm-forecasts-performed)); RCP's competitive-Senate averages were 1.3 pts more Republican than 538's in 2022. Silver Bulletin 2024: Senate average error 4.1 (lowest in its database), ≈3 pts Democratic bias in presidential/Senate/House polls, ≈2 in gubernatorial; across 2016/2020/2024 polls overestimated Democrats by 3.8 pts on average, while the 2018/2022 midterms were "almost completely unbiased" ([Silver](https://www.natesilver.net/p/so-how-did-the-polls-do-in-2024-its), [Silver](https://www.natesilver.net/p/what-if-the-polls-are-wrong-again)). AAPOR 2024 task force: state polls most accurate since 1944, still underestimating Republicans; since the 1930s each party has been underestimated about equally often ([AAPOR report](https://aapor.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/AAPOR-Task-Force-on-2024-Pre-Election-Polling_Report.pdf)). **Shirani-Mehr, Rothschild, Goel & Gelman (2018), *JASA* 113(522): 607–614** – 4,221 polls, 608 state races 1998–2014: election-level absolute bias ≈2.1 pts (Senate) / 2.3 (governor) per a secondary citation; actual error is roughly double the nominal margin of error (exact figures unverified – search budget exhausted). [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:taf:jnlasa:v:113:y:2018:i:522:p:607-614). 2020 AAPOR numbers (national polls overstated Biden by ≈3.9, state by ≈4.3 – recollection, verify).
- **Converting margin to win probability.** Mean absolute error ≈ 0.8σ for a normal, so σ_total ≈ 1.25×MAE: Senate ≈6.5–7.5, Governor ≈6.5, House ≈8–11 (House polls are sparse; use PVI-heavy priors). Use P(win) = T₅(margin/σ) (538 uses t with 5 df). Illustrative Senate values (σ=7): lead 3 → ≈0.66; 5 → ≈0.75; 8 → ≈0.86; 10 → ≈0.91; 15 → ≈0.98. Empirical check: since 1998 Senate candidates leading by >5.5 pts in the average 30 days out were 68–0; leaders by 3–6 pts were 9–3 ([538](https://fivethirtyeight.com/features/the-uncanny-accuracy-of-polling-averages-part-ii-what-the-numbers-say/)). Remaining drift: by mid-October a presidential margin has ≈3 pts of expected movement left ([538 2024](https://abcnews.com/538/538s-2024-presidential-election-forecast-works/story?id=113068753)); midterm generic-ballot averages are steadier (Silver).
- **Fundamentals inputs.** Incumbency advantage has collapsed: **Jacobson (2015), *JOP* 77(3): 861–873** finds it at its lowest since the 1950s due to nationalization; a secondary series shows the House incumbency coefficient falling from ≈4.7 (2000) to ≈1.4 (2022) ([De Gruyter](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/for-2023-2003/html)). Split Ticket's 2026 model: pollster-weighted generic ballot → likely-voter screen → demographic swings projected onto each district/state → incumbency and WAR (candidate quality) adjustments → blend with a model trained on four cycles and race polls → Monte Carlo with correlated national/state/demographic errors; ratings Lean ≥60%, Likely ≥80%, Safe ≥95%; its 2024 Senate model missed 1 of 34 but over-rated Democrats in Florida, safe states and heavily Hispanic areas ([The Argument](https://www.theargumentmag.com/p/split-ticket-2026-midterms-model), [Split Ticket 2024 review](https://split-ticket.org/2024/12/06/how-did-split-tickets-models-perform-in-2024/)). Rothschild (2009): polls overstate margins and are anti-incumbent; discount leads toward the fundamentals (Erikson & Wlezien).
- **Directional hedge.** Presidential-year polls under-estimated Republicans three cycles running; midterm polls (2018, 2022) were unbiased, and 538's research says direction is unpredictable ex ante. 2026 is a midterm under a Republican president; the standard midterm pattern favors the out-party, which the generic ballot should already reflect.

**=> Trading implication.** Fair value per race = T₅((poll average adjusted toward fundamentals − house effects)/σ_type), with σ_Senate ≈7, σ_Gov ≈6.5, σ_House ≈9 (PVI + generic ballot + incumbency prior when polls are absent), plus a national bias draw with σ≈2.5 and *zero* mean (a mild Republican-lean prior is defensible but should carry little weight). Where the market price differs from this by more than ~5¢ (and the gap is not explained by a hard event you missed), trade it with limit orders. Because the dominant uncertainty is a shared national error, keep the net party exposure within the Section B cap.
